I am going to say something that most governance vendors will not say:

Our product will not prevent every bad thing your AI does.

No PII detection catches every pattern. A customer who spells out their ID number in words bypasses every regex and most ML classifiers. No policy engine prevents every hallucination. LLMs are stochastic — you cannot guarantee that a model will never produce a sentence you did not anticipate.

Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling a feeling, not a product.

A seatbelt does not prevent car crashes. It does not make you a better driver. What it does: when the crash happens, it dramatically reduces the damage. It converts a fatal outcome into a survivable one.

AI governance is a seatbelt.

When something goes wrong, you end up in one of two positions:

Position A: "We had no idea." No logs, no audit trail, no evidence.

Position B: "We caught it. Here is the cryptographic receipt showing what was scanned, what was detected, and what action was taken."

The difference is not whether the incident happened. It is whether you can prove you were prepared.

Three layers make this work: detection (catch 99%+), evidence (cryptographic receipts for everything), and response (humans take over when AI fails). No single layer is sufficient. All three together make you defensible.
