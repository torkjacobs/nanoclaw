Unpopular opinion: Your AI assistant doesn't need more features. It needs governance.

I've watched teams spend months fine-tuning prompt chains, building RAG pipelines, and optimizing latency — only to pull the plug before launch because legal couldn't sign off.

The pattern is always the same:

1. Build the cool AI thing
2. Demo it to leadership
3. Get asked "what happens if it leaks customer data?"
4. Scramble to add guardrails
5. Realize the architecture doesn't support them
6. Start over, or ship with risk

Governance isn't the opposite of speed. It's what makes speed sustainable.

When PII detection runs in ~20ms, when every response is checked against compliance rules before it reaches the customer, when the system knows when to escalate — that's not overhead. That's the foundation that lets you ship with confidence.

The teams deploying AI customer-facing successfully in 2025 aren't the ones with the most sophisticated models. They're the ones who treated governance as architecture, not afterthought.
